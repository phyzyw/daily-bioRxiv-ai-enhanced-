Abstract: Hydrogen-deuterium exchange mass spectrometry (HDX-MS) provides powerful insight into protein structure and dynamics, but interpretation of protection patterns at atomic resolution often requires complementary computational modeling. We present PHENOMS, an open-source Python package that integrates molecular dynamics trajectories with HDX-MS data to analyze backbone hydrogen-bond networks by tracking how individual hydrogen bonds and residue-residue contacts form, break, and reorganize throughout the simulations. This residue-level analysis can reveal local structural changes that may be missed by conventional global trajectory metrics. PHENOMS employs a multithreaded, Rust-accelerated computational engine that achieved an average 16-fold speedup (up to 100-fold) over widely used Python-based MD analysis packages in our benchmarks, while retaining individual hydrogen-bond resolution in every trajectory frame. We demonstrate PHENOMS through multiple retrospective case studies using published and in-house datasets, showing that it can recapitulate HDX-MS protection patterns, identify key residues that drive coordinated structural changes, and benchmark the computational efficiency of the approach. With automated quality-control workflows, PHENOMS provides a framework for integrating hydrogen-bond analysis with HDX-MS data or serves as a standalone, high-throughput tool for analyzing MD trajectories for broader applications in protein engineering, drug discovery, and structural biology.
