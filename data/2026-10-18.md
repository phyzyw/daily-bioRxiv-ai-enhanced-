Abstract: Structural colour provides durable, pigment-free colouration by controlling the interaction of light with nanostructured materials rather than by using wavelength-selective pigments. In birds, non-iridescent blue and green plumage arises from coherent scattering by quasi-ordered keratin-air nanostructures, making these natural systems attractive models for sustainable colour engineering. Despite decades of research on their optical mechanisms, open end-to-end implementations that connect biological nanostructures with colour prediction, inverse design and physics-based evaluation remain limited. Here I present Kawasemi Colours, an open end-to-end platform for structure-colour design based on avian structural colour. From published electron micrographs, I assembled a provenance-tracked dataset of spongy, channel and quasi-ordered sphere nanostructures with associated colour metadata. I extracted interpretable structural descriptors, developed compact forward models that predict colour from nanostructure images, and implemented an analytic inverse design tool that generates candidate structures for a target colour. Both directions are provided through self-contained interactive web applications. Kawasemi Colours provides an open framework for studying and designing structural colour from biological nanostructures. By integrating curated data, interpretable machine learning, wave optics, and interactive design tools, the platform lowers the barrier to comparative structural-colour research and provides a cumulative foundation for testing and refining structure-colour relationships as new biological data become available.

Abstract: Enzyme catalyzed reactions drive metabolism and enable diverse applications in biotechnology and sustainable synthesis, but assigning catalytic functions to uncharacterized enzymes and finding enzymes for target reactions remain major challenges. Despite recent advances in machine learning, translating enzyme function prediction into systematic discovery across the vast space of uncharacterized proteins and biochemical reactions remains challenging. Here we present EzHit, a lightweight framework for bidirectional enzyme reaction retrieval. Across enzyme annotation, orphan-reaction retrieval and family-specific fine-tuning benchmarks, EzHit achieved strong top-ranked performance and outperformed representative enzyme reaction matching baselines. We further show that Mahalanobis distance based geometric confidence provides more useful reliability estimates than probability-derived uncertainty metrics or evidential learning, enabling confidence-aware prioritization of high scoring candidates. Owing to its efficiency, EzHit was applied to a nearly trillion-scale OMG_Prot50 with RHEA screen, yielding 8.20 billion retained enzyme reaction associations across 30.27 million predicted enzyme sequences. Experimental validation further confirmed the activity of EzHit prioritized metagenomic enzyme candidates for a sparsely annotated reaction. Together, these results establish EzHit as a scalable framework for enzyme annotation, reaction-guided mining and biocatalyst discovery

Abstract: Personalized neoantigen based vaccines have progressed from concept to clinical evidence for some of the most aggressive human tumors, yet a key challenge remains: determining which candidates should be prioritized for vaccine inclusion. Public neoantigens, shared across tumor subtypes and patient populations, offer potential as off the shelf candidates for broad spectrum immunotherapy. Candidate selection relies on in silico pipelines that estimate binding; however, binding affinity alone is an imperfect marker of immunogenicity. Molecular dynamics (MD) combined with endpoint free energy methods estimates binding free energy directly from simulated trajectories, and hydrogen mass repartitioning (HMR) mitigates the computational cost of hydrogen involving bond vibrations. This study assessed whether HMR accelerated MD can prioritize immunogenic neoantigens through peptide HLA binding free energy calculations and quantified the trade off between accuracy and computational cost. Ten immunogenic public neoantigens (seven paired with their wild type counterparts and three frameshift neoantigens analyzed as unpaired references) were modeled in complex with their HLA I alleles, built de novo using AlphaFold3 and propagated with a 4 fs HMR time step. Mean binding free energy was computed across ten replicas per complex, and each SNV pair was scored concordant with experimental data when the neoantigen formed a more favorable complex than its wild type. Free energies were recomputed at 100 to 500 ns to identify the shortest trajectory preserving reliable ranking. Free energy calculations reproduced experimental concordance for six of seven SNV pairs and were inconclusive for TP53 R8H. Frameshift neoantigens yielded comparable values. Most complexes retained both peptide termini anchored to the HLA groove throughout, and RMSD and RMSF metrics agreed with one another and with the free energy rankings. The PIK3CA E545K (E11K) pair showed the most distinctive signature: the peptides engaged the groove almost identically over P1 to P8, with the entire difference confined to the C terminal pocket, where loss of the wild type's anchor also disrupted its backbone anchor. This pair showed the largest free energy separation in the panel, and restricting analysis to bound frames confirmed a substantial electrostatic and van der Waals advantage favoring the neoantigen. These findings support HMR accelerated binding free energy MD as a practical refinement step for neoantigen prioritization and its application to assessing public neoantigens for cancer vaccine design.

Abstract: Actin nucleation is a rate-limiting step in F-actin assembly. However, early actin trimers are intrinsically unstable, with limited inter-subunit contacts rendering them prone to dissociation. VASP is an important regulator of actin polymerization and assembly, but how it stabilizes unstable trimeric actin nucleus remains poorly understood at the molecular level. Here, we use coarse-grained and all-atom molecular dynamics simulations to characterize the interfacial stability and conformational features of VASP-bound actin trimers. Energetic and interfacial analyses show that VASP preferentially anchors the laterally associated subunit through multivalent interactions and reinforces its interfaces with the longitudinal actin dimer. Within the VASP-bound trimer, the three actin subunits retain distinct local conformational features, as reflected by differences in W-loop gating and SD1-to-SD4 dihedral angle. Across replicate simulations, VASP stabilizes the actin nucleus while preserving subunit-specific conformational heterogeneity, rather than uniformly rigidifying the trimer. These findings provide a molecular basis for VASP-mediated stabilization of early actin nuclei and offer broader mechanistic insights into the regulation of protein assembly.
